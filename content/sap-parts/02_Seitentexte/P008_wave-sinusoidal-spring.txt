Wave / Sinusoidal Spring

Technical introduction

Wave and sinusoidal spring elements can provide compact axial loading where installation space is limited. The actual load curve must be checked for each design.

Selection and engineering checks

A correct replacement needs more than a similar picture. Confirm the dimensions, construction and material version.

• Available axial space.

• Spring load-versus-deflection curve. Check actual spring force at the installed working height.

• Cyclic movement.

• Corrosion resistance. Include the full chemical composition and its concentration.

• Seal face geometry.

Common failure mechanism

Assuming that two spring shapes have the same force at the same height can lead to incorrect face loading.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Axial loading and installation

The spring acts together with hydraulic pressure on the effective seal area. Check its working compression at the specified installation length; excessive compression can increase heat generation, whereas insufficient closing force can compromise face tracking. Direction-sensitive coil springs also require a verified rotation direction.

Material pairing and surface integrity

Specify the rotating and stationary faces as a pair. Hardness alone does not determine suitability: thermal conductivity, friction behaviour, corrosion resistance, abrasives, surface flatness and lubrication regime also matter. Evaluate the actual product grade and manufacturing route before substitution.

Stationary interface and seating geometry

Confirm seat outside diameter, axial thickness, installation depth, anti-rotation method, elastomer seat and housing tolerance. The counterface must remain square to the shaft and must not be distorted by uneven mounting force. A visually similar ring can have a different functional geometry.

Technical information we need

For an application-specific recommendation, please send: existing drawing or seal reference; shaft diameter and mating seat dimensions; pressure and temperature at the seal; fluid composition, contaminants and solids; speed, operating cycle and shaft movement; present material configuration and observed failure pattern.
