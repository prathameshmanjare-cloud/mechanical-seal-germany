Split Cartridge

Technical introduction

Split cartridge designs can be installed around a shaft when removing the drive or coupling would be difficult. Actual split lines and sealing interfaces vary by product.

Selection and engineering checks

A correct replacement needs more than a similar picture. Confirm the dimensions, construction and material version.

• Shaft condition.

• Installation access.

• Split joint.

• Concentricity.

• Speed. Calculate relevant sliding speed and review starts and stops.

• Seal chamber limits.

Common failure mechanism

Dirt or a damaged split joint can cause immediate leakage even when the face materials are correct.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Cartridge assembly and setting

A preassembled cartridge can reduce field-setting errors when installed as specified, but it does not eliminate interface checks. Verify shaft sleeve, gland connection, set screw positions, flush and barrier connections, installation tabs, rotation and dimensional clearances before removing setting devices.

Hydraulic loading and pressure transients

Use the pressure at the seal chamber, including start-up, shutdown, pressure reversal and pulsation—not simply the maximum rated pump pressure. Face balance ratio and support-system pressure affect frictional loading and leakage. Pressure protection or alternative seal chamber geometry requires engineering verification.

Material pairing and surface integrity

Specify the rotating and stationary faces as a pair. Hardness alone does not determine suitability: thermal conductivity, friction behaviour, corrosion resistance, abrasives, surface flatness and lubrication regime also matter. Evaluate the actual product grade and manufacturing route before substitution.

For an application-specific recommendation, please send: existing drawing or seal reference; shaft diameter and mating seat dimensions; pressure and temperature at the seal; fluid composition, contaminants and solids; speed, operating cycle and shaft movement; present material configuration and observed failure pattern.
