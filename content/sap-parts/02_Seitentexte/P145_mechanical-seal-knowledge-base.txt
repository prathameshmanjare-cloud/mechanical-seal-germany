Mechanical Seal Knowledge Base

Technical introduction

Clear technical explanations for selecting, installing and maintaining mechanical seals.

Our Main Areas

Select a topic below to learn about its technical requirements and available SAP Parts support. - How Mechanical Seals Work: A mechanical seal controls leakage where a rotating shaft passes through a stationary housing. A rotating face works against a stationary face. Secondary seals close other leakage paths. - Seal Types & Designs: Mechanical seals can be grouped by spring or bellows construction, cartridge assembly and single or dual arrangement. Pusher, non-pusher, balanced and unbalanced are technical features. - Seal Face Materials Explained: Common faces are carbon graphite, silicon carbide, tungsten carbide and aluminium oxide. The material grade, binder and application determine suitability. - Elastomers & Chemical Compatibility: Elastomer O-rings and bellows prevent leakage away from the primary faces. Typical families include NBR, EPDM, FKM, HNBR and FFKM. Compound formulation matters. - Material Pairing Selection: A material pairing has to support lubrication, thermal behaviour and resistance to the process medium. Popular combinations serve different conditions rather than a universal ranking. - Spring Force & Face Loading: The spring provides preload, while hydraulic pressure also acts on the seal. The resulting face load influences leakage, heat and wear. - Hydraulic Balance: Hydraulic balance reduces the area on which sealed pressure creates closing force. It is a design method for controlling face loading at pressure. - Dry Running & Lubrication: Dry running means that a sufficient lubricating fluid film is absent. Many standard seals can fail quickly when face temperatures rise. - Failure Analysis: Leakage is a symptom, not a root cause. Inspection should consider damage to faces, O-rings, springs, drive parts and the surrounding pump system. - Installation & Common Mistakes: Correct installation is essential for mechanical seal life. Clean parts, correct shaft dimensions and the specified installed length matter as much as material selection. - Maintenance & Troubleshooting: Maintenance should identify changing leakage, heat, vibration, noise and fluid condition before a small problem becomes a pump failure. - Seal Lifetime & Total Cost of Ownership: The purchase price is only one part of a sealing system cost. Downtime, labour, spare parts, lost product and additional energy can be much more expensive.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Material pairing and surface integrity

Specify the rotating and stationary faces as a pair. Hardness alone does not determine suitability: thermal conductivity, friction behaviour, corrosion resistance, abrasives, surface flatness and lubrication regime also matter. Evaluate the actual product grade and manufacturing route before substitution.

Engineering a material decision

A material change should begin with a failure mechanism and measurable acceptance criteria. Evaluate the face pair, corrosion behaviour, thermal properties, geometry, production route and total installed cost. A lower purchase price is useful only when fit, reliability and required qualification are maintained.

Verification and traceability

Define acceptance criteria that are meaningful for each component: material identity, critical dimensions, face geometry, elastomer specification and functional checks where applicable. Inspection records should identify the relevant production batch and drawing revision. Product-specific certificates require actual supporting evidence.

Technical information we need

For an application-specific recommendation, please send: existing drawing or seal reference; shaft diameter and mating seat dimensions; pressure and temperature at the seal; fluid composition, contaminants and solids; speed, operating cycle and shaft movement; present material configuration and observed failure pattern.

Engineering enquiry

Send us the seal reference, equipment data, drawing or dimensions. SAP Parts reviews the fit, operating duty and material pairing before proposing a verified replacement or an engineered alternative.
