Technical introduction

Industrial heat pumps use refrigerants and thermal circuits with repeated changes in operating conditions. Seals must match the exact machine and working fluid.

Technical Overview

Industrial heat pumps use refrigerants and thermal circuits with repeated changes in operating conditions. Seals must match the exact machine and working fluid.

Selection and engineering checks

The following points help define the correct sealing solution and its limits.

• Refrigerant.

• Oil.

• Temperature. Check continuous and start-up temperatures at the sealing interface.

• Pressure cycles. Use the actual pressure at the seal chamber and note transients.

• Leak requirement.

• Start-stop pattern.

Common Risks

A good material can still fail when the design or operating conditions are wrong. A high-temperature elastomer may not be suitable for the selected refrigerant.

Engineering approach

SAP Parts reviews the operating duty, checks the available dimensions and material options, and identifies the information needed before a qualified solution can be proposed. The aim is reliable operation with a practical total cost.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Refrigerant and thermal-cycle conditions

Heat pump components may encounter variable pressure, temperature and phase conditions. Select materials based on the specific refrigerant, lubricants and cleaning agents and examine dynamic shaft behaviour. Seal qualification must match the machine architecture.

Hydraulic loading and pressure transients

Use the pressure at the seal chamber, including start-up, shutdown, pressure reversal and pulsation—not simply the maximum rated pump pressure. Face balance ratio and support-system pressure affect frictional loading and leakage. Pressure protection or alternative seal chamber geometry requires engineering verification.

Secondary seal material selection

Check chemical compatibility against the complete fluid mixture, cleaning agents, exposure time and operating temperature. Consider swelling, compression set, extrusion gaps and dynamic movement where applicable. The polymer name by itself does not define the performance of the finished compound.
