Selection and engineering checks

The following points help define the correct sealing solution and its limits.

• Fiber content.

• Solids.

• Corrosion. Include the full chemical composition and its concentration.

• Process pressure. Use the actual pressure at the seal chamber and note transients.

• Agitation.

• Seal cooling.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Fibres, deposits and gas/liquid transitions

Biogas slurry often contains fibres, suspended solids and deposits that can obstruct dynamic secondary seals. Check gas pressure, process chemistry, agitation direction, seal location and the operating state when the shaft stops. The engineering solution may involve a protected seal location, modified materials or a different arrangement.

Mixing duty and shaft dynamics

Agitator seals must tolerate mechanical movement that can be very different from pump service, including long shafts, radial deflection and changes in vessel pressure. Check seal position, bearing arrangement, gas or liquid exposure, rotation direction and access for maintenance. The sealing arrangement must be matched to the actual vessel duty.

Engineering a material decision

A material change should begin with a failure mechanism and measurable acceptance criteria. Evaluate the face pair, corrosion behaviour, thermal properties, geometry, production route and total installed cost. A lower purchase price is useful only when fit, reliability and required qualification are maintained.
