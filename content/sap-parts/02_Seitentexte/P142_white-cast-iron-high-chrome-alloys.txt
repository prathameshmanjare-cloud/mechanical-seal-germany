White Cast Iron & High-Chrome Alloys

Technical introduction

High-chromium white cast irons contain hard carbides in an iron-based matrix and can provide abrasion resistance at competitive manufacturing cost. Grade, heat treatment and final finish matter.

Technical Principle

High-chromium white cast iron contains hard carbide phases within an iron-based matrix. The type and distribution of carbides influence abrasion resistance, while the matrix influences toughness, corrosion response and machining behaviour.

Materials And Design

Casting quality, alloy composition and heat treatment are important. A ring made from the right nominal alloy can still fail if porosity, residual stress or poor surface finishing affect the sealing interface. The final face must meet the specified flatness and roughness.

Engineering Conditions

For some abrasive duties, casting-based wear surfaces may offer a competitive manufacturing route. The material can also be compared with tungsten carbide coatings, silicon carbide or other alloys. Such comparisons require identical operating conditions and agreed performance targets.

Reliability And Cost

White cast iron, high-chromium cast iron and Ni-resist are not interchangeable names. They are different material families or grades. SAP Parts considers these differences when developing a cost-optimised sealing design.

Selection and engineering checks

• Chromium content.

• Carbide distribution.

• Corrosion. Include the full chemical composition and its concentration.

• Toughness.

• Casting quality.

• Grindability.

• Pairing.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Casting route and final machining

Centrifugal and other casting routes can influence structure, inclusions and machining stock. Specify alloy chemistry, heat treatment, functional surfaces and final inspection before selecting a casting method. Cast blanks must still meet dimensional and surface requirements after machining.

Engineering a material decision

A material change should begin with a failure mechanism and measurable acceptance criteria. Evaluate the face pair, corrosion behaviour, thermal properties, geometry, production route and total installed cost. A lower purchase price is useful only when fit, reliability and required qualification are maintained.

Manufacturing tolerances and functional faces

Process planning must link the turning, grinding and lapping stages to the finished seal geometry. Flatness, squareness, concentricity, roughness and dimensional control matter differently at different interfaces. Establish measurement methods and lot traceability for critical dimensions.
