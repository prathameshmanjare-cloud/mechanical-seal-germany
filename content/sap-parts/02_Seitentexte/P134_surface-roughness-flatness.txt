Surface Roughness & Flatness

Technical introduction

Seal faces need suitable flatness and surface finish to control leakage and support lubrication. Flatness and roughness are different properties and must be measured separately.

Technical Principle

Seal face flatness controls how evenly the rotating and stationary surfaces meet. Surface roughness describes smaller-scale peaks and valleys. Both influence the microscopic lubricating film and potential leakage paths.

Materials And Design

Precision faces are often lapped. Flatness can be checked using an optical flat and monochromatic light; interference bands reveal differences in surface height. The number of acceptable bands depends on the seal design, material and specification.

Engineering Conditions

A polished ring is not automatically flat. High temperature, poor mounting or incorrect press fits can deform the face after manufacturing. Surface damage may also be introduced during handling and assembly.

Reliability And Cost

The finished face must be evaluated as part of the complete seal. A ring that passes a bench inspection may distort when installed or exposed to pressure and heat. The developer image should show these different scales without copying third-party diagrams.

Selection and engineering checks

• Lapping process.

• Optical flat.

• Light-band inspection.

• Surface texture.

• Thermal distortion.

• Material. Specify the grade and pairing rather than a broad material family.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Material pairing and surface integrity

Specify the rotating and stationary faces as a pair. Hardness alone does not determine suitability: thermal conductivity, friction behaviour, corrosion resistance, abrasives, surface flatness and lubrication regime also matter. Evaluate the actual product grade and manufacturing route before substitution.

Engineering a material decision

A material change should begin with a failure mechanism and measurable acceptance criteria. Evaluate the face pair, corrosion behaviour, thermal properties, geometry, production route and total installed cost. A lower purchase price is useful only when fit, reliability and required qualification are maintained.

Lubrication limits and thermal risk

The term dry running covers different situations: temporary liquid loss, a gas-filled interface or an intentionally gas-lubricated design. Conventional liquid-lubricated seals may overheat rapidly without the intended fluid film. Define the duration, frequency and environmental conditions before selecting face materials or special geometry.
