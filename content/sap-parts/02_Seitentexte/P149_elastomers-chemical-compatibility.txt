Elastomers & Chemical Compatibility

Technical introduction

Elastomer O-rings and bellows prevent leakage away from the primary faces. Typical families include NBR, EPDM, FKM, HNBR and FFKM. Compound formulation matters.

Technical Principle

The secondary seal closes the path between the rotating assembly and shaft, or between the stationary seat and housing. O-rings, rubber bellows and gaskets have to match the entire process fluid, including cleaners and additives.

Materials And Design

NBR is often considered for mineral oils and certain general industrial duties. EPDM is widely used for water-based systems and some hot-water duties but is generally unsuitable for many petroleum oils. FKM can be useful for numerous oils and chemicals, while hot-water, amine or polar-fluid compatibility depends strongly on the precise compound. HNBR, PTFE-based components and FFKM provide other options for specific requirements.

Engineering Conditions

Temperature limits are not universal numbers for a polymer family. Compound formulation, cure system, contact duration, pressure, compression, aging and dynamic movement all matter. A compound that works as a static gasket may be unsuitable as a dynamic secondary seal.

Reliability And Cost

Ask for the real product composition, cleaning media, temperature cycles and required approval. Use the exact compound specification and documented compatibility, not only a generic material abbreviation.

Selection and engineering checks

• Exact fluid. Check lubrication, vapor pressure and compatibility with the support fluid.

• Additives.

• Cleaning agents.

• Continuous temperature. Check continuous and start-up temperatures at the sealing interface.

• Swelling.

• Compression set.

• Certification requirements.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Secondary seal material selection

Check chemical compatibility against the complete fluid mixture, cleaning agents, exposure time and operating temperature. Consider swelling, compression set, extrusion gaps and dynamic movement where applicable. The polymer name by itself does not define the performance of the finished compound.

Corrosion and fluid properties

Chemical identity alone may be insufficient: concentration, impurities, temperature and cleaning stages can change compatibility. Verify corrosion resistance of faces, elastomers and metal parts, as well as lubrication, viscosity and vapor-pressure risk. Mixed service should be evaluated across the full operating cycle.

Engineering a material decision

A material change should begin with a failure mechanism and measurable acceptance criteria. Evaluate the face pair, corrosion behaviour, thermal properties, geometry, production route and total installed cost. A lower purchase price is useful only when fit, reliability and required qualification are maintained.
