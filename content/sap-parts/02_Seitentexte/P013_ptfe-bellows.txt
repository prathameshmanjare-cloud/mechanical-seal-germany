PTFE Bellows

Technical introduction

PTFE bellows designs are considered when resistance to aggressive chemicals is a key requirement. Their stiffness and deformation under load differ from elastomer bellows.

Selection and engineering checks

A correct replacement needs more than a similar picture. Confirm the dimensions, construction and material version.

• Chemical concentration.

• Temperature. Check continuous and start-up temperatures at the sealing interface.

• Pressure. Use the actual pressure at the seal chamber and note transients.

• Creep.

• Installation dimensions.

• Face materials. Specify the grade and pairing rather than a broad material family.

Common failure mechanism

PTFE can deform over time under load. A chemical-resistant material is not automatically a mechanically suitable design.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Secondary sealing and axial flexibility

A bellows may supply spring action, provide secondary sealing or perform both functions, depending on the construction. Flexibility, fatigue resistance, medium compatibility and temperature cycling must be assessed together. Do not assume a rubber, PTFE and welded-metal bellows have the same pressure and temperature capability.

Material pairing and surface integrity

Specify the rotating and stationary faces as a pair. Hardness alone does not determine suitability: thermal conductivity, friction behaviour, corrosion resistance, abrasives, surface flatness and lubrication regime also matter. Evaluate the actual product grade and manufacturing route before substitution.

Secondary seal material selection

Check chemical compatibility against the complete fluid mixture, cleaning agents, exposure time and operating temperature. Consider swelling, compression set, extrusion gaps and dynamic movement where applicable. The polymer name by itself does not define the performance of the finished compound.
