Dry Running & Lubrication

Technical introduction

Dry running means that a sufficient lubricating fluid film is absent. Many standard seals can fail quickly when face temperatures rise.

Technical Principle

A conventional liquid-lubricated mechanical seal normally needs a stable film between the faces. During dry running or poor lubrication, the film cannot carry the normal load, so friction and temperature may rise rapidly.

Materials And Design

Possible causes include empty suction lines, trapped air, low liquid level, hot fluid that flashes to vapour, blocked circulation and an incorrect seal arrangement. A pump may contain liquid while the local sealing interface has poor lubrication.

Engineering Conditions

Some specialised materials and seal designs can tolerate defined dry-running periods. This must be proven for the stated pressure, speed, temperature and duration. It does not mean the same seal is suitable for continuous dry operation.

Reliability And Cost

Prevention begins with operating data and system design: venting, priming, cooling, monitoring and suitable support fluids. If dry operation is an unavoidable part of the duty, it must be treated as a design requirement from the start.

Selection and engineering checks

• Dry period.

• Surface speed. Calculate relevant sliding speed and review starts and stops.

• Face force.

• Ambient cooling.

• Material thermal properties. Specify the grade and pairing rather than a broad material family.

• Start/stop pattern.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Material pairing and surface integrity

Specify the rotating and stationary faces as a pair. Hardness alone does not determine suitability: thermal conductivity, friction behaviour, corrosion resistance, abrasives, surface flatness and lubrication regime also matter. Evaluate the actual product grade and manufacturing route before substitution.

Engineering a material decision

A material change should begin with a failure mechanism and measurable acceptance criteria. Evaluate the face pair, corrosion behaviour, thermal properties, geometry, production route and total installed cost. A lower purchase price is useful only when fit, reliability and required qualification are maintained.

Lubrication limits and thermal risk

The term dry running covers different situations: temporary liquid loss, a gas-filled interface or an intentionally gas-lubricated design. Conventional liquid-lubricated seals may overheat rapidly without the intended fluid film. Define the duration, frequency and environmental conditions before selecting face materials or special geometry.
