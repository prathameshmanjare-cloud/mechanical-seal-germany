Stationary Seal Seats

The stationary seal seat is the fixed mating component of a mechanical seal. Its face runs against the rotating ring while its outside geometry and secondary sealing arrangement locate and seal it inside the housing.

Function inside the seal

The stationary seat must provide a stable, flat mating face and remain correctly positioned against hydraulic and mechanical loads. The primary face pair controls leakage through a very thin running film; the secondary seal blocks a bypass path between seat and housing.

Mounting geometry matters

O-ring-mounted, elastomer-cup, L-profile and interference-fit seats are different mounting systems. Outside diameter, depth, shoulder, chamfer, anti-rotation provision and housing tolerances can be as important as the face material. Substituting a seat based only on the visible sealing-face diameter can damage the housing or lead to leakage.

Material selection

Alumina ceramics, silicon carbide and tungsten carbide are widely used counterface options. Relevant properties include corrosion resistance, thermal conductivity, fracture behaviour and abrasive wear resistance. A hard seat is not automatically the best solution: the complete face pairing and operating liquid determine suitability.

Common causes of leakage

A tilted or chipped seat, distorted elastomer cup, contaminated seating bore or incorrect interference can compromise face alignment. Thermal growth and pressure loading can change the installed condition. Examine the housing and mating ring before declaring a seat interchangeable.
