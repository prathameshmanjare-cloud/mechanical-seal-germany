Friction & Wear

Technical introduction

Tribology is the study of friction, lubrication and wear between surfaces in relative motion. The two seal faces experience changing contact conditions during start-up, running and shutdown.

Technical Overview

Selection and engineering checks

The following points help define the correct sealing solution and its limits.

• Material pairing. Specify the grade and pairing rather than a broad material family.

• Contact load.

• Sliding velocity.

• Fluid lubricity. Check lubrication, vapor pressure and compatibility with the support fluid.

• Surface state.

• Cooling.

Common Risks

A good material can still fail when the design or operating conditions are wrong. A low-friction material alone does not solve a seal with an unstable fluid film or poor geometry.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Material pairing and surface integrity

Specify the rotating and stationary faces as a pair. Hardness alone does not determine suitability: thermal conductivity, friction behaviour, corrosion resistance, abrasives, surface flatness and lubrication regime also matter. Evaluate the actual product grade and manufacturing route before substitution.

Engineering a material decision

A material change should begin with a failure mechanism and measurable acceptance criteria. Evaluate the face pair, corrosion behaviour, thermal properties, geometry, production route and total installed cost. A lower purchase price is useful only when fit, reliability and required qualification are maintained.

Lubrication limits and thermal risk

The term dry running covers different situations: temporary liquid loss, a gas-filled interface or an intentionally gas-lubricated design. Conventional liquid-lubricated seals may overheat rapidly without the intended fluid film. Define the duration, frequency and environmental conditions before selecting face materials or special geometry.
