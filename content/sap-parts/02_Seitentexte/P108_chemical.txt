Selection and engineering checks

The following points help define the correct sealing solution and its limits.

• Concentration.

• Additives.

• Pressure and temperature. Use the actual pressure at the seal chamber and note transients.

• Evaporation.

• Corrosion. Include the full chemical composition and its concentration.

• Emissions limits.

Common Risks

A good material can still fail when the design or operating conditions are wrong. Unknown solvent content can swell an otherwise suitable O-ring.

Engineering detail — how we solve the problem

Corrosion and fluid properties

Chemical identity alone may be insufficient: concentration, impurities, temperature and cleaning stages can change compatibility. Verify corrosion resistance of faces, elastomers and metal parts, as well as lubrication, viscosity and vapor-pressure risk. Mixed service should be evaluated across the full operating cycle.

Engineering a material decision

A material change should begin with a failure mechanism and measurable acceptance criteria. Evaluate the face pair, corrosion behaviour, thermal properties, geometry, production route and total installed cost. A lower purchase price is useful only when fit, reliability and required qualification are maintained.

Secondary seal material selection

Check chemical compatibility against the complete fluid mixture, cleaning agents, exposure time and operating temperature. Consider swelling, compression set, extrusion gaps and dynamic movement where applicable. The polymer name by itself does not define the performance of the finished compound.
